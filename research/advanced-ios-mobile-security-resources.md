# Advanced iOS/mobile security training and challenge resources

**Verified:** 2026-04-14  
**Scope:** legitimate, authorized application security, reverse engineering, and userland vulnerability research. Prices are the public page values seen on the verification date; tax, promotions, lab access, and checkout totals can change.

## Bottom line

For a pwn.college-style, challenge-first learner, do **not** begin by buying a long survey course. A stronger sequence is:

1. **Start with the free challenge banks:** [Mobile Hacking Lab (MHL) iOS labs](https://www.mobilehackinglab.com/hacking-labs.html), [8kSec iOS Application Exploitation Challenges](https://academy.8ksec.io/course/ios-application-exploitation-challenges), and the [OWASP iOS crackmes/reference apps](https://mas.owasp.org/crackmes/iOS/). They provide the closest match to flag-oriented learning; MHL's [No Escape](https://academy.mobilehackinglab.com/course/lab-no-escape) and [Captain No Hook](https://academy.mobilehackinglab.com/course/lab-captain-nohook) pages explicitly require retrieving flags with Frida/reversing.
2. **Add +Ch0pin's actual project, Medusa, as a tool—not as a course.** Use it alongside OWASP/8kSec targets, then work through [NVISO's Frida iOS Playground](https://github.com/NVISOsecurity/frida-ios-playground) for focused instrumentation drills.
3. **If paying for structure:** choose [8kSec Practical Mobile Application Exploitation](https://academy.8ksec.io/course/practical-mobile-application-exploitation) for app pentesting, or [8kSec Offensive iOS Internals](https://academy.8ksec.io/course/offensive-ios-internals) / [Corellium iOS Reverse Engineering](https://www.corellium.com/ios-reverse-engineering-training) for deeper iOS internals and reversing. Confirm device/lab entitlement before purchase.
4. **Only choose a native exploit-development track deliberately.** [MHL iOS Userland Fuzzing & Exploitation](https://www.mobilehackinglab.com/courses/ios-userland-fuzzing.html) is userland 0-click research, not ordinary appsec and not iOS kernel exploitation; as checked, it was still a pre-sale.

## Who/what is “+Ch0pin”?

**+Ch0pin is the handle of Dimitrios Valsamaras.** His own author profile identifies “+Ch0pin,” links the `valsamaras` identity, and calls him the developer of [`Ch0pin/medusa`](https://github.com/Ch0pin/medusa); a Microsoft research post is signed “Dimitrios Valsamaras.” ([author profile](https://valsamaras.medium.com/android-security-workshop-5eadeb50fba), [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2024/05/01/dirty-stream-attack-discovering-and-mitigating-a-common-vulnerability-pattern-in-android-apps/), [GitHub profile](https://github.com/Ch0pin))

The relevant project is **Medusa (Mobile Edge-Dynamic Unified Security Analysis)**, a Frida-backed modular runtime-testing framework for Android and iOS. Its repository documents iOS startup via `medusa_ios.py`, modules for SSL pinning, jailbreak/root bypass, WebViews and deep links, and a rooted target plus Frida-server requirement; it had a commit on 2026-04-10, so it was active at verification. It is **free tooling, not a challenge curriculum**. ([README](https://github.com/Ch0pin/medusa), [2026-04-10 commit](https://github.com/Ch0pin/medusa/commit/f90073033d48747a470d146dba0d0682fdc819a1))

Two corrections matter:

- +Ch0pin's published **Android Security Workshop** is Android material, not iOS training. ([workshop](https://valsamaras.medium.com/android-security-workshop-5eadeb50fba))
- [`Ch0pin/Reverse-Engineering-Tutorial`](https://github.com/Ch0pin/Reverse-Engineering-Tutorial) is a 2021 fork, 960 commits behind its upstream when checked, and its README says it was assembled by `@0xInfection`; it should not be treated as current +Ch0pin-authored iOS material. ([repository](https://github.com/Ch0pin/Reverse-Engineering-Tutorial))

No primary source found a current iOS course or iOS challenge set authored by +Ch0pin. Mobile Hacking Lab sponsors Medusa, but that is not evidence that he authored MHL's courses. ([Medusa README](https://github.com/Ch0pin/medusa))

## Appsec/reversing versus exploit development

- **Application security/reversing:** IPAs, Swift/Objective-C logic, Keychain/storage, URL schemes, WebViews, TLS pinning, Frida hooks, app patching and backend/API behavior. Start with MHL/8kSec/OWASP challenges; 8kSec Practical, Bai7/Sven Schleier, or SANS are structured extensions.
- **iOS userland vulnerability research:** native libraries and system services, fuzzing, crash triage, ARM64 memory corruption and userland exploit chains. MHL Userland and Corellium iOS RE fit here; this is closer to pwn.college, but it is not ordinary mobile pentesting.
- **iOS kernel/jailbreak research:** XNU/kernelcache, kernel debugging, PAC/PPL/SPTM and kernel read/write or sandbox-escape chains. 8kSec iOS Internals/OMRE teach architecture and case-study analysis but explicitly stop short of production-grade kernel-exploit construction. Corellium's separate [Intro to iOS Kernel Tools](https://www.corellium.com/resources/courseware-training/ios-vulnerability-training-course) is a short team course ($5,000 virtual half-day; $15,000 on-site full-day with labs), not a pwn.college-like challenge track. Do not buy an appsec course expecting kernel exploit development.

## High-signal researcher-led material

- **Jiska Classen — Practical iOS Reverse Engineering at OffensiveCon:** unusually deep four-day training spanning apps, system internals, IOKit, firmware/RTKit, tooling automation, and current iOS behavior. It uses either a jailbroken physical iPhone or Corellium and explicitly includes challenging exercises for intermediate/advanced attendees. This is one of the strongest verified researcher-led options, but it is scheduled conference training rather than a self-serve challenge platform. ([OffensiveCon training](https://www.offensivecon.org/trainings/2026/practical-ios-reverse-engineering.html), [research profile](https://www.seemoo.tu-darmstadt.de/team/jclassen/))
- **Google Project Zero iOS write-ups:** primary-source exploit analyses such as FORCEDENTRY are excellent worked case studies after userland/ARM64 foundations, but they are research publications—not packaged labs. ([Project Zero](https://projectzero.google/), [FORCEDENTRY analysis](https://projectzero.google/2021/12/a-deep-dive-into-nso-zero-click.html))
- **Apple Security Research:** Apple's security portal publishes platform research and runs the Security Research Device program for qualified researchers. This is a later-stage research route, not beginner training or a general bug-bounty lab. ([Apple Security Research](https://security.apple.com/), [SRD program](https://security.apple.com/research-device/))
- **Ivan Rodriguez — RE-iOS-Apps:** a free course built around vulnerabilities reported in production apps, with setup, IPA extraction, static/dynamic analysis, and patching. The core methodology is legitimate, but much of the material dates to 2019 and includes obsolete tooling, so adapt it to Frida/LLDB rather than copying commands blindly. ([repository](https://github.com/ivRodriguezCA/RE-iOS-Apps))

## Suggested challenge-first route

1. **Zero-cost triage:** solve OWASP UnCrackable L1/L2 without spoilers; then pick the advanced MHL iOS flags and 8kSec's Frida/runtime-restricted challenges.
2. **Instrumentation depth:** complete NVISO Frida iOS Playground manually, then repeat selected targets using +Ch0pin's Medusa modules and finally write equivalent raw Frida hooks.
3. **Broaden appsec:** use MASTG tests against iGoat-Swift, DVIA-v2 and iBugBazaar; keep notes as a pentest report. Consider the MHL CIPT practical only if the reporting credential is useful.
4. **Choose a specialization:** 8kSec Practical for assessment breadth; 8kSec iOS Internals/Corellium for internals and reversing; MHL Userland only for native fuzzing/exploit development after its pre-sale content and final price can be verified.

## Currency and exclusions

- OWASP's index can call a reference app “current” even when the app's own README names an old tested iOS version; expect re-signing/build fixes.
- NVISO Frida Playground remains useful but has not changed since 2023. Past conference deliveries such as [DeepSec 2025's Mobile Playbook](https://blog.deepsec.net/deepsec-2025-training-the-mobile-playbook-a-guide-to-ios-and-android-app-security-hybrid-in-person-or-online-sven-schleier/) documented Corellium labs and a final CTF, but no 2026 public date was verified; Bai7 still lists the training, with price/schedule on request. ([Bai7](https://www.bai7.at/))
- No current hands-on iOS course/challenge was verified from r2con/NowSecure; the primary pages found were a [2024 conference retrospective](https://www.nowsecure.com/blog/2024/11/15/nowsecure-at-r2con2024-top-takeaways-and-mobile-security-highlights/) and an [iOS research Q&A](https://www.nowsecure.com/blog/2020/08/20/qa-adventures-in-ios-app-security-research/), not an active lab offering.
- “Corellium device,” “virtual iPhone,” and browser cloud device mean **virtualized iOS**, not a physical iPhone, regardless of nearby “real device/environment” marketing language.
